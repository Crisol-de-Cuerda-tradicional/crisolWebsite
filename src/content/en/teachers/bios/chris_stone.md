Chris Stone is an Australian fiddler, composer, educator and artistic director whose work brings together folk traditions, chamber music, composition and improvisation. Raised in Australia's folk festival community, he has developed a musical practice grounded in listening, collaboration and the shared experience of making music with others.

Chris is a founding member of the acclaimed chamber-folk trio **The String Contingent**, with whom he spent more than a decade touring throughout Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and Scandinavia, releasing six albums of original music. Shaped by the landscapes, communities and musical traditions of Australia, his work explores questions of place, identity and belonging. Through composition, improvisation and collaboration, he is interested in music as a conversation between people, places and experiences.

Alongside his work with **The String Contingent**, Chris orchestrated the Noongar-language operas *Koolbardi wer Wardong* and *Wundig wer Wilura* for West Australian Opera, created by Noongar artist **Gina Williams** and **Guy Ghouse**. *Koolbardi wer Wardong* was the first full-length opera performed entirely in an Australian Indigenous language. These landmark works deepened Chris's exploration of culture, language, storytelling and custodianship, and continue to shape his understanding of the relationship between music, community and place.

Chris holds a PhD from the Australian National University in violin improvisation, researching aural learning and alternative approaches to fingerboard navigation. As an educator, he is passionate about ear-based learning and helping musicians develop confidence, creativity and musical fluency through listening, experimentation and making music together. He has taught at camps and workshops throughout Australia and internationally, including Scotland's **Skye Fiddle School**.

Chris is founder and artistic director of **Stringmania**, one of Australia's leading residential string music camps, and Co-Artistic Director of the **National Folk Festival**.
